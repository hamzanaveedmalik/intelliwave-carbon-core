# Website Analysis Instructions

Step-by-step guide for the sales-marketing agent to conduct comprehensive website analyses.

## Overview

This document provides instructions for analyzing websites using the 15-section framework. The goal is to identify improvement opportunities that will enhance conversion rates, user experience, and business outcomes.

## Pre-Analysis Setup

### 1. Gather Information
Before starting the analysis, collect:
- **Website URL**: The site to analyze
- **Business context**: What does the business do?
- **Target audience**: Who are their customers?
- **Business goals**: What are they trying to achieve?
- **Current challenges**: Any known issues?
- **Competitors**: Who are their main competitors?

### 2. Initial Site Visit
- Visit the website in an incognito/private browser window
- Test on desktop and mobile devices
- Note first impressions
- Check multiple pages (homepage, pricing, about, etc.)

### 3. Tools to Use
- **Browser DevTools**: For performance analysis
- **Google PageSpeed Insights**: For speed and Core Web Vitals
- **Lighthouse**: For comprehensive audit
- **GTmetrix**: For performance testing
- **Screaming Frog**: For SEO audit (if available)
- **SimilarWeb/Alexa**: For competitive data (if available)

## Analysis Process

### Step 1: Initial Assessment (5 minutes)
1. Visit the homepage
2. Note immediate impressions
3. Can you understand what they do in 3 seconds?
4. Is the value proposition clear?
5. What's your first impression of trust/credibility?

### Step 2: Framework Analysis (30-45 minutes)
Go through each of the 15 sections systematically:

#### Section 1: Value Proposition Analysis
- **Questions to ask:**
  - What is the main value proposition?
  - Is it clear within 3 seconds?
  - Is it benefit-focused or feature-focused?
  - How does it differentiate from competitors?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Headline clarity
  - Supporting messaging
  - Proof points
  - Differentiation

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Generic messaging
  - Feature-focused instead of benefit-focused
  - No clear differentiation
  - Value prop buried below the fold

#### Section 2: Hero Section Evaluation
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Is the headline compelling?
  - Is the CTA prominent?
  - Does the visual support the message?
  - Is it mobile-friendly?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Headline quality
  - CTA placement and design
  - Visual quality
  - Mobile responsiveness
  - Above-the-fold content

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Weak headlines
  - Hidden CTAs
  - Poor visuals
  - Mobile issues

#### Section 3: CTA Optimization
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Are CTAs action-oriented?
  - Are they placed strategically?
  - Do they match user intent?
  - Are there too many or too few?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - CTA copy quality
  - Placement throughout site
  - Design and visibility
  - Context relevance

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Generic CTAs ("Learn more")
  - Too many competing CTAs
  - Poor visibility
  - Context mismatch

#### Section 4: Social Proof & Trust Signals
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Are testimonials present and credible?
  - Are customer logos visible?
  - Are there case studies?
  - Are trust badges present?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Testimonial quality and placement
  - Customer logos
  - Case studies
  - Trust badges
  - Usage statistics

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Generic testimonials
  - No customer logos
  - Missing case studies
  - Trust signals buried

#### Section 5: Content Structure
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Is content scannable?
  - Is the hierarchy clear?
  - Is information easy to find?
  - Is content appropriately detailed?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Heading structure
  - Content organization
  - Readability
  - Information architecture

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Walls of text
  - Poor heading structure
  - Missing information
  - Confusing organization

#### Section 6: Pricing Presentation
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Is pricing easy to find?
  - Are plans clearly differentiated?
  - Is value communicated?
  - Are there trial/demo options?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Pricing visibility
  - Plan clarity
  - Comparison tools
  - Value communication

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Hidden pricing
  - Unclear plans
  - No value justification
  - Missing trial options

#### Section 7: Conversion Optimization
- **Questions to ask:**
  - What friction points exist?
  - Are forms too long?
  - Is navigation clear?
  - Are there unnecessary steps?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Form complexity
  - Navigation clarity
  - Conversion path
  - Friction points

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Long forms
  - Confusing navigation
  - Too many steps
  - Mobile friction

#### Section 8: SEO & Discoverability
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Are title tags optimized?
  - Is there proper heading structure?
  - Are images optimized?
  - Is the site fast?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Title tags and meta descriptions
  - Heading structure
  - Image optimization
  - Page speed
  - Mobile-friendliness

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Missing title tags
  - Poor heading structure
  - No alt text
  - Slow loading

#### Section 9: UX Analysis
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Is the interface intuitive?
  - Is design consistent?
  - Is it accessible?
  - Does it work well on mobile?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Navigation clarity
  - Design consistency
  - Mobile experience
  - Accessibility
  - Visual hierarchy

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Confusing navigation
  - Inconsistent design
  - Poor mobile UX
  - Accessibility barriers

#### Section 10: Competitive Positioning
- **Questions to ask:**
  - How is this site differentiated?
  - What unique advantages are communicated?
  - Are competitive comparisons provided?
  - Is positioning consistent?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Differentiation messaging
  - Competitive comparisons
  - Positioning clarity
  - Message consistency

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Generic positioning
  - No differentiation
  - Inconsistent messaging
  - Missing comparisons

#### Section 11: Mobile Experience
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Is it fully responsive?
  - Are touch targets large enough?
  - Is navigation mobile-friendly?
  - Does it load quickly on mobile?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Responsive design
  - Touch target sizes
  - Mobile navigation
  - Mobile performance
  - Mobile CTAs

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Not fully responsive
  - Small touch targets
  - Difficult navigation
  - Slow mobile loading

#### Section 12: Content Quality & Depth
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Is content comprehensive?
  - Is it valuable and actionable?
  - Is it current?
  - Does it answer key questions?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Content depth
  - Value provided
  - Currency
  - Completeness

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Thin content
  - Outdated information
  - Missing key info
  - No unique value

#### Section 13: Trust & Credibility
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Is contact info easy to find?
  - Is there an About page?
  - Are legal pages present?
  - Does it feel professional?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Contact information
  - About page
  - Legal pages
  - Professionalism
  - Security indicators

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Hidden contact info
  - Missing About page
  - No legal pages
  - Unprofessional appearance

#### Section 14: Performance & Technical
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Does it load quickly?
  - Are there broken links?
  - Is SSL enabled?
  - Are Core Web Vitals good?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Page load speed
  - Broken links
  - SSL certificate
  - Core Web Vitals
  - Error rates

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - Slow loading
  - Broken links
  - No SSL
  - Poor Core Web Vitals

#### Section 15: Analytics & Measurement
- **Questions to ask:**
  - Is analytics installed?
  - Are goals tracked?
  - Are key events tracked?
  - Is there regular reporting?
  
- **What to look for:**
  - Analytics presence
  - Goal tracking
  - Event tracking
  - Reporting setup

- **Common issues to identify:**
  - No analytics
  - Missing goal tracking
  - Incomplete event tracking
  - No reporting

### Step 3: Competitive Comparison (15 minutes)
If competitors are provided:
1. Visit competitor sites
2. Compare key elements side-by-side
3. Identify what competitors do better
4. Identify what the analyzed site does better
5. Note opportunities for differentiation

### Step 4: Prioritization (10 minutes)
1. List all identified issues
2. Categorize by:
   - Critical (blocking conversions)
   - High priority (significant impact)
   - Medium priority (important but not urgent)
   - Low priority (nice to have)
3. Identify quick wins (low effort, high impact)

### Step 5: Documentation (15 minutes)
Create the analysis report using the output template.

## Questions to Ask During Analysis

### About the Business
- What is the primary business goal for the website?
- Who is the target audience?
- What are the main conversion goals?
- What are the biggest challenges?

### About Current Performance
- What is the current conversion rate?
- What is the bounce rate?
- What pages perform best/worst?
- What are users complaining about?

### About Competitors
- Who are the main competitors?
- What do competitors do better?
- What is the competitive advantage?
- How should the site differentiate?

## Common Issues to Identify

### Critical Issues (Fix Immediately)
- No clear value proposition
- Missing or hidden pricing
- No contact information
- Broken primary CTA
- Site doesn't load
- Not mobile-friendly
- No SSL certificate

### High Priority Issues
- Generic or weak headlines
- No social proof
- Slow page load (> 5 seconds)
- Poor mobile experience
- Confusing navigation
- Missing trust signals
- No clear next steps

### Medium Priority Issues
- Thin content
- Poor SEO basics
- Inconsistent design
- Missing FAQ
- No case studies
- Weak CTAs
- Poor form design

## Best Practices to Reference

### Value Proposition
- Lead with customer outcomes, not features
- Use specific, quantifiable benefits
- Test multiple variations
- Align messaging across touchpoints

### CTAs
- Use action verbs
- Create urgency when appropriate
- Match CTA to page context
- Test button colors, sizes, and copy

### Social Proof
- Include photos, names, titles for testimonials
- Display recognizable customer logos
- Show specific results in case studies
- Place trust signals near CTAs

### Mobile
- Mobile-first design approach
- Large touch targets (minimum 44x44px)
- Fast loading (< 3 seconds on 3G)
- Simplified forms for mobile

### Performance
- Optimize images (compress, WebP format)
- Minimize HTTP requests
- Enable browser caching
- Use CDN for static assets

## Analysis Tips

1. **Be Objective**: Focus on data and best practices, not personal preferences
2. **Be Specific**: Provide concrete examples and recommendations
3. **Be Actionable**: Every finding should have a clear recommendation
4. **Prioritize**: Not all issues are equal - focus on impact
5. **Be Constructive**: Frame issues as opportunities for improvement
6. **Use Evidence**: Reference best practices, benchmarks, or data when possible
7. **Consider Context**: Understand the business goals and target audience
8. **Think Mobile-First**: Always consider mobile experience
9. **Focus on Conversion**: Keep business goals in mind
10. **Provide Examples**: Show what good looks like when possible

## Next Steps After Analysis

1. **Present Findings**: Share the analysis report with stakeholders
2. **Prioritize Actions**: Work with the team to prioritize improvements
3. **Create Implementation Plan**: Break down recommendations into actionable tasks
4. **Set Up Tracking**: Ensure analytics are in place to measure improvements
5. **Schedule Follow-up**: Plan to review progress and iterate
